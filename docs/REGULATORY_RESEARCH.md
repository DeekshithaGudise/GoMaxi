## Executive summary

1. A 12-seat Tempo Traveller is a **maxi cab** (MV Act s.2(22)) and, when used for hire, a **public service vehicle** that needs a **contract carriage permit** under s.74. **[VERIFIED]**
2. The statutory definition of contract carriage (s.2(7)) requires hire of the vehicle "as a whole" on a fixed/agreed sum and "without stopping to pick up or set down passengers not included in the contract". Only a *motor cab* (≤6 passengers) is expressly saved "notwithstanding that separate fares are charged"; a *maxi cab* is **not** given that saving. Selling individual seats on a fixed metro-to-tech-park route is the textbook pattern that Karnataka's Transport Department has treated as unlicensed **stage carriage** operation (Ola Shuttle / ZipGo seizures, 2016–17). **[VERIFIED] + [RISK]**
3. Vehicles permitted as **educational institution buses** or school cabs (Karnataka 2012 Rules) are permitted/taxed for carrying students and staff only; using them for fare-paying public passengers needs a separate contract carriage permit, different registration/tax class, and removal of school livery. **[VERIFIED (definition)] + [LEGAL INTERPRETATION]**
4. Any app that connects passengers to drivers for hire is an **aggregator** under s.93 MV Act. Karnataka licenses aggregators under the **Karnataka On-demand Transportation Technology Aggregators Rules, 2016**; MoRTH's **Motor Vehicles Aggregator Guidelines 2025** (1 July 2025) update the national template. GoMaxi will need a Karnataka aggregator licence before dispatching a single vehicle. **[VERIFIED (requirement)]**
5. Per-vehicle gating items: contract carriage permit, commercial (yellow-board) registration, fitness certificate, PUC, third-party insurance covering passengers, AIS-140 VLT + panic button (now tied to fitness renewal in Karnataka), speed governor; per-driver: transport-endorsed DL, Karnataka driver's badge (Rule 12A KMVR), police verification, medical fitness. **[VERIFIED/ASSUMPTION mix, see below]**
6. Passenger data: DPDP Act 2023 is being phased in (Rules notified 13 Nov 2025; core fiduciary obligations from ~13 May 2027); until then IT Act s.43A / SPDI Rules 2011 still apply. Build consent, retention and breach workflows now. **[VERIFIED (secondary)]**

## 1. Maxi cab classification and permit

- **[VERIFIED]** MV Act 1988 s.2(22): "maxi cab" means any motor vehicle constructed or adapted to carry **more than six passengers, but not more than twelve passengers, excluding the driver**, for hire or reward. s.2(25) "motor cab" = not more than six passengers. s.2(35) "public service vehicle" includes a maxi-cab, a motor-cab and a contract carriage. s.2(47) "transport vehicle" = public service vehicle, goods carriage, educational institution bus or private service vehicle. Source: Odisha Transport Dept copy of the Act — https://odishatransport.gov.in/mvach1
- **[VERIFIED]** s.66(1): no owner shall use or permit use of a vehicle as a transport vehicle in a public place except under a permit. s.74 governs contract carriage permits; the RTA may attach conditions including maximum hiring rates and passenger numbers. Sources: MoRTH copy of Chapter V — https://morth.gov.in/sites/default/files/MV-Act-1988-Chapter-5.pdf ; https://devgan.in/mva/chapter_05.php
- **[VERIFIED (secondary)]** s.192A (as amended 2019): using a vehicle without a permit — fine up to ₹10,000 for first offence, imprisonment possible for repeat. https://www.caseon.in/section/section-192a-the-motor-vehicles-act-1988
- **[ASSUMPTION — CONFIRM]** Maxi cab contract carriage permits in Karnataka are issued by the jurisdictional RTO (Bengaluru East/KR Puram etc.); fees and the standard permit conditions (route-free, "whole vehicle" hire) were not found on transport.karnataka.gov.in during this research. Confirm fee schedule and conditions with the RTO.

## 2. Contract carriage vs stage carriage — the core risk

- **[VERIFIED]** s.2(7) "contract carriage": vehicle engaged "under a contract … for the use of such vehicle **as a whole** … on a fixed or an agreed rate or sum … (a) on a time basis … or (b) from one point to another; and in either case, **without stopping to pick up or set down passengers not included in the contract anywhere during the journey**, and includes (i) a maxicab; and (ii) a motor-cab **notwithstanding that separate fares are charged** for its passengers". s.2(40) "stage carriage": vehicle carrying more than six passengers "for hire or reward **at separate fares paid by or for individual passengers**, either for the whole journey or for stages of the journey". https://odishatransport.gov.in/mvach1
- **[LEGAL INTERPRETATION]** GoMaxi's model — unrelated passengers, individual per-seat fares, fixed origin (metro station) to fixed hotspot, repeated throughout the day — matches every element of s.2(40). The "separate fares" saving in s.2(7)(ii) is written for motor cabs only; counsel must advise whether a maxi cab charging per seat can still be a contract carriage. Courts treat en-route pick-up/drop as the sharpest dividing line (Kerala HC on tourist buses — https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/kerala-high-court/kerala-high-court-tourist-buses-not-allowed-pick-up-drop-off-passengers-like-stage-carriage-269295 ), but one older Madras-era line of authority held separate fares alone are not conclusive. Mitigation options to put to counsel: (a) single pick-up at station, single drop at hotspot, no intermediate stops; (b) structuring the booking as a pre-formed group "virtual contract" (the model MoRTH's aggregator guidelines use for pooling); (c) seeking stage carriage permits (practically foreclosed — see below); (d) partnering with BMTC rather than competing.
- **[VERIFIED — enforcement precedent]** 30 Mar 2017: Karnataka Transport Dept SIT seized two Ola "Shuttle" buses and two ZipGo Tempo Travellers; officials said picking up general commuters at stops breached permit rules, only BMTC may run public shuttles in the city, and recommended revoking Ola's contract carriage permit. Ola removed Shuttle from its app within an hour. https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/bengaluru-officials-seize-2-ola-shuttles-operating-illegally-want-revoke-licence-59494 ; earlier notice to Ola for offering "shuttle" in 10+2 seat vehicles under its aggregator licence — https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/ola-gets-notice-shuttle-service-2001923 ; ZipGo asked to suspend — https://inc42.com/?p=38532
- **[VERIFIED — enforcement precedent]** Sept–Oct 2023 carpooling crackdown: dept warned of RC suspension up to 6 months and ₹5,000–10,000 fines for white-board cars on pooling apps; minister later said no ban but apps "must take permissions and operate within the legal framework". https://inc42.com/buzz/transport-dept-tightens-noose-on-illegal-carpooling-in-bengaluru/ ; https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fbengaluru%2Fno-plans-to-ban-carpooling-but-apps-must-operate-as-per-law-says-minister-2709815
- **[VERIFIED — precedent]** Bike taxis: single judge (2 Apr 2025) held aggregators cannot operate bike taxis until the State notifies guidelines under s.93; services stopped 16 Jun 2025; Division Bench (CJ Vibhu Bakhru & J. C.M. Joshi, 23 Jan 2026) set that aside and directed the State to consider registration as transport vehicles and grant contract carriage permits, while letting the State add licence conditions under its existing aggregator rules. Lesson: Karnataka regulates through the s.93/KODTTA licence and will litigate novel models. https://www.thenewsminute.com/karnataka/karnataka-rapido-ola-uber-must-stop-bike-taxis-within-6-weeks-says-hc ; https://www.thenewsminute.com/karnataka/karnataka-hc-lifts-bike-taxi-ban-directs-state-govt-to-grant-permits
- **[RISK]** BMTC holds the practical monopoly on intra-city stage carriage in Bengaluru; a private stage carriage permit on these corridors is not a realistic path. Expect union complaints (as with Ola/ZipGo, carpooling and bike taxis) to trigger enforcement.

## 3. School / educational-institution vehicles

- **[VERIFIED]** s.2(11): "educational institution bus" means an omnibus **owned by** a college, school or other educational institution and used **solely** for transporting its students or staff. https://odishatransport.gov.in/mvach1

- **[VERIFIED (secondary)]** Supreme Court, M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (16 Dec 1997): school buses yellow, "School Bus" marking, speed control device, first-aid box, fire extinguisher, attendant, reliable door locks. Judgment copy: https://jkhighcourt.nic.in/doc/upload/judgments/2023/sci/S_1997_6_522_527.pdf ; summary: https://kashmirlife.net/guidelines-for-safety-of-school-buses-to-be-adhered-27555/
- **[VERIFIED (court-reported)]** Karnataka taxes institution-owned buses under a separate "educational institution bus" item of the KMVT Act Schedule (Item 8(f)(ii) in the Canadian International School case), distinct from public service vehicles. https://judgments.vakilsearch.com/principal-canadian-international-school-vs-state-karnataka-others/63bf46e225ccf301531c01d3
- **[LEGAL INTERPRETATION]** Two populations exist: (a) institution-owned buses (s.2(11)) — cannot carry the public at all without re-classification, re-taxation and a public service permit; (b) privately owned "school cabs" that already hold a s.74 contract carriage permit — these are legally contract carriages, but the school-children Rules' livery/speed-governor conditions and any school-specific permit endorsement must be examined; a 40 km/h governor and yellow "School cab" body are incompatible with public commuter service. Counsel/RTO must confirm whether such a permit can be used for non-school hire and whether a permit variation is required.
- **[ASSUMPTION — CONFIRM]** We could not find the KMVT Act concessional rate text for educational institution buses; assume a concession exists and that public fare-paying use would forfeit it and trigger tax reassessment/penalty.
- **[RISK]** Operating a school-liveried vehicle for public fares is a visible, easily enforced violation and a reputational risk with schools and parents.

## 5. Insurance

- **[VERIFIED (secondary)]** Chapter XI: s.146 makes third-party insurance compulsory; s.147 requires the policy to cover death/bodily injury to passengers of a public service vehicle (contract and stage carriages). https://www.caseon.in/section/section-146-the-motor-vehicles-act-1988 ; https://www.casemine.com/in/column/section-147-of-the-motor-vehicles-act,-1988-–-scope,-limits-and-judicial-construction/view ; https://www.latestlaws.com/latest-caselaw/2007/february/2007-latest-caselaw-170-sc/
- **[LEGAL INTERPRETATION]** The insurer's passenger liability tracks the vehicle's **permitted use**; if a contract carriage is used as a stage carriage, insurers routinely plead breach of permit conditions to avoid passenger claims. This is a direct financial consequence of the s.2(7)/s.2(40) issue.
- **[VERIFIED (secondary)]** MVAG 2025: aggregator to provide drivers health cover ≥₹5 lakh and term cover ≥₹10 lakh; vehicles need third-party and driver-protection cover. https://authbridge.com/blog/2025/08/08/

## Implications for GoMaxi's operating model (candid)

**(a) Per-seat pooled fares on fixed routes vs contract carriage permits.** This is the existential issue. The statute defines stage carriage by exactly what GoMaxi proposes — separate fares from individual passengers for the whole or stages of a journey — and withholds from maxi cabs the "separate fares" saving it gives motor cabs. Karnataka's Transport Department has acted on precisely this theory against Ola Shuttle and ZipGo, both of which used Tempo Travellers on contract carriage permits. Unless counsel can structure each departure as a single whole-vehicle contract with a pre-formed group (and the RTO/STA accepts that in writing, ideally as a permit condition or aggregator-licence condition), GoMaxi should assume that dispatching per-seat rides is unlicensed stage carriage: vehicle seizure, s.192A penalties, permit cancellation, and insurers disputing passenger claims. A legally cleaner near-term path is B2B: whole-vehicle contracts with employers/tech parks for named employee groups departing from metro stations, with GoMaxi as aggregator/fleet manager. A direct-to-consumer product should not launch without written regulatory comfort.

**(b) School-permit vehicles.** Institution-owned buses cannot carry the public at all. Operator-owned school cabs hold contract carriage permits but are liveried, governed to 40 km/h and conditioned for school use; repurposing them for public fares needs permit review, livery change, re-taxation and probably a different vehicle. Their idle-daytime economics are attractive but the compliance delta is large; treat them as a later-phase supply source after RTO confirmation, not as launch fleet.

**(c) Aggregator licence.** GoMaxi is an aggregator under s.93 the moment its app matches a passenger to a driver for a fare. A Karnataka aggregator licence (2016 Rules, possibly updated to MVAG 2025) is mandatory before launch; the licence conditions (control room, police-verified drivers with badges, Kannada/residency conditions, AIS-140 vehicles, fare caps, data obligations, driver insurance) define the product's minimum viable compliance. Whether the current Karnataka licence covers 12-seat maxi cabs is unconfirmed and must be asked directly of the Transport Commissioner; Karnataka has previously objected to a 10+2 "shuttle" offered under a cab aggregator licence.

**Other gating facts.** Every vehicle needs: yellow-board registration, valid contract carriage permit, fitness (2y/2y/annual after 8y), PUC, third-party insurance naming passenger liability, AIS-140 VLT + panic button (now a fitness precondition), speed governor, age ≤8 years for onboarding. Every driver needs: transport-endorsed DL, Karnataka badge, police verification, medical, induction training. The platform needs DPDP-ready notice/consent, retention and breach workflows (SPDI compliance meanwhile), and a women's-safety stack (trip sharing, SOS, women-only pooling option, control room). None of these are optional, and none of them cure the permit-category problem in (a).
